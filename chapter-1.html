Chapter 1

Sight Unseen

I didn't set out to find a cabin. I set out to find a place where I could live what I'd spent years studying.

At the time I was working in Wyoming. My background is sustainability and the built environment. I have a bachelor's in that field and an associate's degree in architecture. I also did two internships with the Department of Energy through the Federal Energy Management Program, known as FEMP. Those internships let me dig into geographical systems, with different layers and maps that told me a lot about where to live and the conditions I wanted to be around for the future. I had also just come back from Germany, where I finished a master's degree in renewable energy engineering and management. I spent my days thinking about how people and land fit together. At some point the question turned personal: where do I want to live that way?

I knew what I wanted. Mountains. Water. Low humidity. A cooler climate. When I put those together on a map, the answer kept pointing to the northwest quadrant of the country.

Then I started ruling places out. Coastal Seattle was too expensive, even though I love the sunsets in Vancouver. Montana was too rural for me. That left northern Idaho.

I went looking on Zillow, and there it was: a half-acre property for $95,000. I bought it in cash, sight unseen. That cash came from risky options trades on the stock market, trades that ended in significant losses. In 2022, the year I bought the property, I lost so much money. But at least I locked in this little piece of land that was mine.

I know how that sounds. All I had to go on was Google Earth. I zoomed around the area to get a feel for it, and what I saw looked like ranch and farm country, not a city.

About six months later, I flew out with a plane ticket and a week-long rental car.

The property had a cabin, and the cabin was run-down. It was not ready to live in. The woman who had lived there before me had clearly been through a rough living situation, and the place showed it. The grass was overgrown, and there were fruit trees, though I had no idea what kind.

I didn't know what animals were around either. I found droppings and wondered if a bear had been through. With so many trees and so much tall grass, I couldn't tell. It turned out deer used to sleep there, and the droppings were theirs.

Standing there, I felt like I was in the middle of the forest. Yet the hospital in town was right next door. It felt wild and close to everything at the same time.

What I didn't know yet was how the cabin worked. I didn't understand any of its systems. Later I found out it ran on solar power, and the batteries were in pretty bad shape. The plumbing was in pretty bad shape too. I had to discover how everything worked and fix it, and my master's degree in renewable energy engineering and management turned out to be exactly what I needed for that.

The first real reminder of how much work lay ahead was the bathroom. The cabin couldn't be lived in yet, so when I needed one, I drove to the nearby casino.

The first stay was rough. There was no heating. There were space heaters in the cabin, but I didn't know how to work them, so I bought a small portable propane heater. I could smell the propane in the room, which wasn't great. But I was still happy to be there. I bought a bottle of wine and celebrated alone.

I didn't stay at the cabin the whole week. I spent a few nights at the Holiday Inn in Spokane, paid for with credit card points, and used those days to recover. Then I'd head back and spend a few days at the cabin.

When I got dirty from the work, I went down to the river to wash off.

Slowly I started making the place livable. I moved some furniture around. The bed that was there was a twin, so I looked on Facebook Marketplace, found a full-size bed, and bought a new mattress to go with it. I also bought plywood to cover some of the exposed insulation in the ceiling, which made the room more comfortable.

That first visit was really about discovering the place and getting comfortable living this far from everyone. Most of my family is in Colombia. My immediate family, like my sister, is in Florida, which is still a long way from Bonners Ferry. Bonners Ferry is only about 30 minutes from the southern border of Canada, so I felt pretty far from everything. I felt like a complete stranger, detached from it all, very far from everyone, just by myself.

But I call this my home.

That was my first week in Bonners Ferry.

It was later, when my mom came to visit, that I found out how little I knew about the trees. She walked off and came back with a plum. I had no idea where she got it. Everything was so overgrown that I couldn't see what was growing out there. There was a plum tree at the bottom of the property that I didn't even know about.

There were more trees than the plum. There are cherry trees now in front of the house that used to be the cabin. Behind the house are crab apple trees. They make tiny apples that taste just like regular apples. I didn't know at first that they were edible, and I found out later that they are.
